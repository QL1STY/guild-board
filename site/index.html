    It reads your account and stays out of your game

    What it reads

    • Your account summary, exploration, resin and Real-Time Notes
    • Your characters, builds, materials and endgame results
    • Your wishes and primogem history, with a read-only key
    • The current banners, events and your check-in month

    What it never does

    • Open the game's process, read its memory or hook into it
    • Send keys or clicks to the game, or capture its screen
    • Change anything in the game's folder
    • Send your HoYoLAB login anywhere but HoYoverse

    A test in the source code fails if any of this stops being true. Read the test, or see how to check the download yourself.

    The risk, plainly

    Guild Board is not made or approved by HoYoverse. Reading a HoYoLAB account with a third-party tool goes against the spirit of its terms. It changes your account in two ways only, when you press the button: redeeming a gift code and claiming the daily check-in. Nobody can promise that an account will never be penalised. You use it at your own risk.

    Questions

    Will I get banned?

    Guild Board never touches the running game, which is what anti-cheat looks for. It reads your account much as the HoYoLAB site does. Still, that is against the spirit of the terms and nobody can promise anything.

    Where does my login go?

    It stays on your PC, encrypted for your Windows user, and goes only to HoYoverse. It never goes online with your friends' features, and never into a log.

    What goes online?

    Nothing, unless you sign in with Discord for friends or sync. Then your name, UID and what you switch on go to the project's database in the EU. Discord is not asked for your e-mail address. You can delete all of it in Settings. Guild Board may also download public pictures from other sites, such as the Endgame headers, and sends nothing of yours with them.

    How do I know the download is what the source says?

    Each installer is built on GitHub from a public commit, with an attestation. With the GitHub command line installed, run gh attestation verify PATH-TO-THE-INSTALLER -R QL1STY/guild-board to check it.

    Its SHA-256 is . In PowerShell, Get-FileHash PATH-TO-THE-INSTALLER shows the same value for the file you downloaded.

    Why does Windows warn about it?

    New programs have no reputation with SmartScreen yet. The warning fades as more people install it.
